Welcome to the Char Dham Pilgrimage Survey!

Namaskar 🙏, we are collecting this survey to get insights into the travel and behavioural aspects of pilgrims at Char Dham, Uttarakhand.

Why we are collecting this information

This survey aims to understand how pilgrims travel to the Char Dham shrines, including their routes, transport modes, travel time, cost, waiting time, vehicle changes, accommodation, and difficulties faced during the journey.

You will also be shown different travel options and asked which one you would prefer. Your answers will help identify where journeys are difficult, costly, or time-consuming and which improvements pilgrims value most.

The findings will support academic research and may help in planning safer, more comfortable, affordable, and reliable transport and facilities for future pilgrims.

Consent Statement

By clicking "I Accept and Agree," you confirm the following:

  • Participation is entirely voluntary.
  • Your responses will be kept anonymous and used solely for academic research and planning purposes.
  • The survey will take approximately 8–10 minutes to complete.

Your honest participation is highly valued and will contribute significantly to improving infrastructure and services for future pilgrims.

Awareness of Char Dham railway improvements:Had you heard about proposed or ongoing rail access improvements before this survey?
Used train for a hill destination before?Tell us if you have used rail travel for any hill or mountain trip.
How do you feel about using the new Rishikesh–Karnaprayag Railway for a future Char Dham trip? The railway is currently under construction and expected to open around 2027–28. It will reach Karnaprayag, from where a feeder bus or taxi will take you to the Dham base.

📋 Please read before you begin

Your current journey: Most pilgrims currently travel from Rishikesh or Haridwar to the Dham route by bus or shared taxi/jeep. This typically takes 7–10 hours (depending on traffic, road conditions, and the destination), and roads can be congested, winding, and weather-dependent during the yatra season.

A new option is being built the Rishikesh–Karnaprayag Railway:

  • A ~125 km mountain railway with 17 stations, currently under construction by the Indian Railways.
  • It is expected to reduce the Rishikesh–Karnaprayag travel time to around 2–3 hours.
  • From Karnaprayag, a feeder bus or taxi will still be needed to reach the Dham base (e.g., Sonprayag for Kedarnath, Joshimath/Badrinath for Badrinath, Govindghat for Hemkund Sahib). The total journey time, including this feeder, is shown in each card.
  • This rail line does not serve the Yamunotri or Gangotri routes so no railway cards are shown for those Dhams.

Your route:   Rishikesh / Haridwar  →  [chosen mode]  →  Dham base town

What each card shows:
  • Cost (₹): Fare per person, one-way, for the entire main-haul journey including feeder.
  • Time (hrs): Total travel time from Rishikesh/Haridwar to the Dham base, including waiting and feeder.
  • Comfort: How comfortable the journey itself feels (seating, space, and fatigue). Low = crowded seats, tiring long ride. Medium = adequate seating, manageable. High = spacious and relaxed.
  • Reliability: How dependable the service is (does it run on time and without cancellation?). Low = frequent delays, diversions, or cancellations. Medium = usually on schedule, minor delays possible. High = consistently on time and predictable.
  • Changes: Number of vehicle / mode changes you need to make during the journey.

📋 Please read before you begin

Your current journey the last mile: Once you reach the base camp or road-head (e.g., Gaurikund for Kedarnath, Gobindghat for Hemkund Sahib, Janki Chatti for Yamunotri), you must still travel up to the shrine itself. Currently, pilgrims choose between trekking on foot, using traditional carriers (pony, mule, doli/palki, porter), or in some locations, taking a helicopter.

A new option is being planned Aerial Ropeways (Gondola / Cable Car):

  • The National Ropeways Development Programme (Parvatmala) proposes ropeways at several Chardham sites, including Kedarnath (Sonprayag–Kedarnath), Hemkund Sahib (Govindghat–Hemkund), and Yamunotri (Janki Chatti–Yamunotri).
  • A ropeway would significantly cut travel time a trek that currently takes 6–9 hours (Kedarnath) could be completed in under 1 hour each way.
  • The ropeway is not yet operational at these sites. This section asks you to imagine it is available at the time of your visit.
What each card shows:
  • Cost (₹): Fare per person, one-way, from the base camp / road-head to the shrine. Trek = ₹0 (but physically demanding). Traditional modes and ropeways have a fare.
  • Time (hrs): One-way travel time from the road-head / base to the shrine.
  • Comfort: How physically demanding and sheltered the journey itself is. Low = strenuous steep trek, fully exposed to weather. Medium = assisted (pony/doli) or partially supported, moderate effort. High = fully seated and sheltered (ropeway cabin), minimal physical exertion.
  • Reliability: Whether the service is available when you arrive and how long you wait before you can start. Low = may be shut (weather, season, or full capacity); long, unpredictable queue before boarding. Medium = mostly available; occasional short waits. High = operates on a regular schedule; minimal wait, rarely disrupted.
